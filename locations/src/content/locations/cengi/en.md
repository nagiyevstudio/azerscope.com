## About the Location

The Jengi (Cəngi) observation spot is located in the Gobustan District of Azerbaijan along the Baku–Shamakhi highway, just 60–70 km west of Baku. It is one of the closest and most accessible dark-sky escapes from the capital, ideal for spontaneous overnight stargazing sessions, telescope meetups, and astrophotography without the need for extensive mountain driving.

The site is set on open hills within the semi-desert steppe, away from direct highway glare and local village illumination.

---

## Astronomical Conditions

- **Bortle Scale:** Class 4.2 (rural/suburban transition sky, SQM ~21.46 mag/arcsec²).
- **Milky Way:** Distinctly visible across the zenith and southern sky on moonless nights.
- **Horizon:** Wide open 360° steppe horizon. While a light dome from the Greater Baku/Sumgayit area is visible to the east, southern, western, and northern skies offer clean observing corridors.
- **Observing Highlights:** Excellent for meteor showers (Perseids, Geminids), lunar & planetary viewing, and bright deep-sky objects (Messier catalogue).

---

## Travel & Logistics

- **Distance from Baku:** ~60–70 km via the paved Baku–Shamakhi highway.
- **Vehicle:** Any standard passenger vehicle (sedan / crossover) in dry weather. Unpaved dirt trails leading off the highway are short and easily navigable.
- **Infrastructure:** Proximity to the main road guarantees strong cellular reception and quick access to nearby gas stations and roadside amenities.

---

## Stargazing Tips

1. **Weather & Soil:** In dry conditions, dirt tracks are easily drivable. Avoid venturing deep into clay soil immediately following heavy rain.
2. **Wind Management:** Open steppe landscapes are prone to evening breezes; position telescopes on leeward hill slopes or use windbreaks.
3. **Temperature & Dew:** Steppe air cools rapidly after sunset; bring warm layers and lens dew heaters.
